The Origin of Video Games



URL Graphics & Games: Timeline of Computer History
Site Author Computer History Museum
Abstract The Computer History Museum's Graphics & Games timeline explains how video games and computer graphics have changed over the years. It goes over some of the earliest computer games, such as OXO and Tennis for Two, along with the development of arcade games, home consoles, and newer gaming technology. The timeline also highlights important inventions and contributions from companies like Atari, Nintendo, and Sony, showing how each played a role in the development of video games. As technology continued to improve, video games became more advanced, with better graphics, new ways to play, and gaming systems that became more accessible to the public. Overall, this source provides a helpful overview of the major developments in gaming history and shows how video games have changed from simple computer-based games into the more advanced and interactive games we are familiar with today.




URL NIMATRON: An Early Electromechanical Machine to Play the Game of Nim
Site Author Jeremy M. Norman
Abstract The History of Information website discusses one of the earliest electronic gaming machines, known as the Nimatron, which was developed by physicist Edward U. Condon in 1940. The machine was created for the New York World's Fair and was designed to play a mathematical strategy game called Nim. The game was originally played by two people taking turns removing objects from piles, but the Nimatron introduced a new way of playing by allowing people to compete against a machine. Instead of using physical objects, the machine used rows of light bulbs and buttons to represent the game. The Nimatron became a popular attraction at the fair, with approximately 100,000 games played against it. Overall, this source helps explain how early gaming technology began to develop and how machines like the Nimatron introduced the idea of people interacting with electronic games.
