The Development of Arcade and Console Gaming



URL Video Game History
Site Author HISTORY.com Editors
Abstract The HISTORY article Video Game History explains how video games developed over time, from early computer games to arcade machines and home gaming consoles. The article discusses how Ralph Baer's invention, known as the Brown Box, eventually led to the release of the Magnavox Odyssey in 1972, which became the first home video game console. It also goes over the development of arcade gaming, including Atari's release of Pong and how its popularity helped make video games more successful with the public. As gaming continued to grow, companies like Atari and Nintendo introduced new consoles and popular games, including Pac-Man, Donkey Kong, and Super Mario Bros. The article also discusses how improvements in graphics, game cartridges, and handheld gaming systems changed the way people played video games, making gaming more accessible and introducing new ways for people to enjoy games both at home and in public spaces.




URL The Father of the Video Game: Video Game History
Site Author Smithsonian Institution
Abstract The Smithsonian Institution's article Video Game History explains how video games developed from early computer games into arcade games and home gaming consoles. The article focuses on Ralph Baer, who began working on the idea of playing video games on a television in 1966. His work eventually led to the creation of the Brown Box, which later became the Magnavox Odyssey in 1972, the first home video game console. The article also goes over the development of arcade gaming, including Nolan Bushnell's creation of Computer Space and Atari's success with Pong. As video games became more popular, companies began releasing new consoles that offered more games and improved technology. The article also discusses some of the challenges the video game industry faced during the early 1980s, along with how Nintendo's release of the Nintendo Entertainment System in 1985 helped bring home gaming back into popularity. This source provides information about some of the major changes in arcade and home console gaming and how these developments helped shape the video game industry.
